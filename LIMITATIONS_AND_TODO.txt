PART A. LIMITATIONS OF THE CURRENT EVALUATION
================================================================

Numbered list of every claim in the Evaluation chapter that is weaker
than it should be, with the reason and the fix.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A1. Single trial per scenario (statistical confidence)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   Each (scenario, condition) was run ONCE.
Effect:   No mean ± stdev. No confidence intervals.
Decision: NOT pursued for this thesis. A seed sensitivity spot check
          on the flood scenario (seed=123456 vs seed=847291653)
          showed that qualitative results -- cycle counts, dominant
          branch distribution, anomaly counts, all three branches
          still firing -- do not change materially across seeds.
          The headline numbers move within roughly +/- 10 percent
          (a small number of bookkeeping artifacts inflate the raw
          BR_RX figure, but external attacker traffic ingestion was
          measured at 878 vs 872 packets across the two seeds, i.e.
          well within noise).  A multi-trial campaign would tighten
          error bars but is not expected to change any qualitative
          claim of the evaluation.
Future:   A 5-trial campaign would still be the right move before
          submitting a journal version of the work, but the cost
          (roughly 15 hours of unattended Sky runs plus parser
          aggregation work) is out of scope for the thesis defence.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A2. Simulation versus real hardware
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   All measurements are MSPSim emulation, not physical Tmote Sky.
Effect:   Energy in mJ is accurate to the model, not to a battery.
          Radio behaviour is UDGM-modelled, not real RF.
What we want: At least one short pilot run on physical hardware to
              calibrate the simulation numbers.
Fix:      Acquire a small set of Tmote Sky boards (or equivalents) and
          re-run the scan scenario for 30 min. Compare BR_ENERGEST
          tick deltas against the simulation.
Effort:   Medium-high. Hardware procurement is the blocker.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A3. Deterministic radio model
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   UDGM treats every link as a disk: in-range succeeds, out-of-
          range fails. No probabilistic loss, no fading, no interference
          from co-located 2.4 GHz devices.
Effect:   PDR numbers are upper bounds. Real deployments will show
          additional baseline loss in BOTH MTD and no-MTD columns.
What we want: Re-run with Cooja's UDGM-DGRM (probabilistic) or MRM
              (more realistic) radio model.
Fix:      Edit the <radiomedium> tag in each .csc file. Pick a
          loss profile (e.g., 95% TX success, 90% RX success) and
          rerun. Compare PDR delta with current numbers.
Effort:   Low (config change), medium (extra runs, restate trial set).

----------------------------------------------------------------
A4. Sinkhole approximation
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   The sinkhole attack is a radio-disruption broadcast storm,
          not a true rank-1 DIO sinkhole that hijacks routes. The CSMA
          MAC's fairness limits how much it can silence sensors.
Effect:   Only 3 silence events fired in the MTD sinkhole run. A true
          DIO sinkhole would produce more. The CONN_FAILURE branch
          fires correctly when it triggers, but the silence rate is
          a lower bound on what a real sinkhole produces.
What we want: A true rank-1 DIO sinkhole built on top of the
              rpl-attacks framework or a custom modification to
              Contiki-NG's RPL-Lite source.
Effort:   High. Requires modifying the routing layer, which would
          contaminate the no-MTD control condition unless carefully
          isolated. May be left to future work.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A5. Selection rule depends on tuning
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   Priority order CONN_FAILURE > CPU_LOAD > STALE_PORT works for
          the attack mix tested. Different mixes (slow stealth scan
          plus brief flood) may favour a weighted scoring rule.
What we want: A small ablation study with two or three alternative
              selection rules: weighted (CONN_FAILURE x 10), threshold
              of 2 instead of 1 for severe types, max-counter (the
              old broken rule, for completeness).
Fix:      Build branch variants of the orchestrator and run each on
          the same logs.
Effort:   Medium.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A6. Coverage of the attack space
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   Three scenarios cover scan, sinkhole, flood. Out of scope:
          insider compromise, link-layer jamming, coordinated multi-
          attacker.
What we want: Optional: a fourth attacker mode for either jamming
              (continuous carrier) or two attackers running different
              modes simultaneously.
Effort:   Medium.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A7. Missing per-packet metrics
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   The current logs contain BR_METRICS at 5-minute intervals,
          which gives 6 data points per run. Detection and reaction
          latencies are computed correctly, but per-packet
          end-to-end latency cannot be derived because we do not log
          the sensor-side TX timestamp alongside the BR-side RX
          timestamp.
What we want: Add a packet-level TX timestamp to the sensor payload
              ("seq=N,port=P,t=T_ms") and have the BR diff it against
              clock_time() at receive.
Attempted but not adopted:
          The change was implemented (sensor payload extended with
          t=, BR parses it, computes per-packet latency, logs the
          aggregate).  The MTD-enabled border router firmware then
          overflowed the Tmote Sky 48 KB flash budget by several
          hundred bytes even after aggressive code-size optimisation
          (collapsing log lines, dropping per-packet RX detail,
          replacing strtoul with atol).  The firmware change was
          reverted; the latency measurement therefore remains a
          deferred work item that would need either a target with
          more flash (e.g. CC2538) or a wider code-size review of
          the existing MTD modules to free up budget.
Effort:   Low (code change is small) but blocked by flash budget on
          the current Tmote Sky target.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A8. Scan success rate and false positive rate not reported
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   The Evaluation chapter reports anomaly counts but not the
          fraction of attacker probes that hit a still-valid address
          (scan success rate) or the fraction of legitimate packets
          incorrectly flagged (false positive rate).
Attempted but not adopted:
          As with A7, the per-source counter implementation (separate
          legit_rx / legit_anomalous / attacker_rx / attacker_anomalous
          buckets keyed on the attacker's well-known IID match) was
          written and integrated, but pushed the BR firmware over the
          Tmote Sky flash budget when combined with the latency change.
          Both items together exceeded the 48 KB ceiling by ~570 bytes
          even after the optimisations described above.  Reverted; see
          the same future-target note in A7.
What we want: Add a per-source counter at the BR (legit vs attacker
              IP prefix) so we can compute both metrics directly from
              the log.
Effort:   Low. One source-IP comparison per packet in the receive
          callback, plus a periodic snapshot.

----------------------------------------------------------------
A9. No reconvergence-time measurement
----------------------------------------------------------------
Status:   We record the times of MTD cycles but not how long after a
          cycle PDR returns to baseline.
What we want: After each shuffle, measure time until BR's RX rate
              returns to its pre-cycle moving average.
Effort:   Medium. Pure post-processing; the data is already in the
          log.

STEP 2.  Multi-trial campaign (closes A1) -- DEFERRED
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Decision: not pursued for the thesis (see A1).
- A seed sensitivity spot check confirmed that the qualitative
  behaviour is stable across random seeds, so a single trial per
  scenario is used.
- The campaign remains a sensible next step before any journal
  submission of the work; the plan is preserved below as future work.
- Plan (if/when pursued):
    * Use 5 random seeds, e.g. 1, 12345, 67890, 314159, 271828.
    * Shell wrapper that loops over seeds, edits the .csc, runs
      Cooja in batch mode, saves the log with a seed suffix.
    * Run all 30 simulations (6 scenarios x 5 seeds) unattended
      overnight (~15 h).
    * Update parse_logs.py to aggregate mean +/- stdev across trials.
    * Re-run make_figures.py to add error bars to the bar charts.
    * Update the Evaluation chapter tables and figures.

----------------------------------------------------------------
- Create three orchestrator variants: weighted, threshold-of-2,
  max-counter.
- Run each through the three attack scenarios (single trial each, no
  baselines needed).

  matches the design intent across all three scenarios.
- Document the chosen rule in Section 5 (System Design).
- Estimated time: 1 day.

- Switch radiomedium to UDGM probabilistic with TX 0.95 / RX 0.90.

  plus the three baselines.
- Compare PDR delta with the current numbers.

================================================================
PART C. NICE-TO-HAVES (probably out of scope for the thesis)
================================================================

- TSCH MAC instead of CSMA. TSCH would change the collision dynamics
  enough that the sinkhole approximation might silence sensors more
  reliably. Out of scope for this thesis but worth a paragraph in
  future work.
- Cryptographically secure PRNG for the IID shuffler. The current
  random_rand() is good enough for an experimental setting; a
  production deployment would substitute ChaCha20 or similar.
- Authenticated CMD_PORT_HOP / CMD_ADDR_SHUFFLE. The current
  commands are unauthenticated UDP, which means an attacker that
  joins the network at the MAC layer could inject false hops.
  Adding HMAC or AES-CCM is a small change but adds RAM cost.
- Power-aware shuffle scheduling. The current proactive timers fire
  on a fixed schedule. A power-aware policy would skip cycles when
  the battery is low.
